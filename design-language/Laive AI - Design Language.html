01 — Principles

Five ideas behind every decision

When a choice is unclear, these resolve it. They are ranked: earlier principles win ties.

01

Outcome over ornament

Every element earns its place by advancing the visitor toward a decision. Beauty is a by-product of clarity, never a substitute for it.

02

Dark, with gold intent

Near-black warm ink is the canvas; gold is spent like currency — on the one thing that matters per view. Scarcity is what makes it read as premium.

03

Depth you can feel

Glass, specular highlights and true 3D transforms give the interface material weight. Surfaces refract, lift toward the cursor, and cast honest shadows.

04

Kinetic, never restless

Motion communicates — a reveal, a scrub, a magnetic pull. It responds to the visitor's input. It never loops for decoration and always yields to reduced-motion.

05

Precision & warmth

Monospaced data, tight geometric headlines and a 4px grid deliver engineering credibility — warmed by copper texture, soft ink and generous, humane spacing. Rigorous, not cold.

Clear space & scale
  • Keep clear space of at least half the mark's height on all sides.
  • Minimum size 24px (UI) / 16px (favicon outline).

  • Place the 3D copper mark on busy or light backgrounds.

05 — Motif & iconography

The hexagon, everywhere

The six-sided cell from the mark is the system's connective tissue — icon frames, bullets, step nodes, badges. Icons are monoline (1.75–2px), 24px grid, rounded joins, drawn in a single stroke weight.

Hex usage rules

Filled gold hex = active / primary. Soft gold hex = feature accent. Neutral hex = inert step. Copper hex = trust & governance. One filled hex per view maximum.

07 — Motion

Choreography with intent

Motion is a language of easing curves and durations. Everything decelerates (ease-out); interactive elements overshoot slightly (spring). Signature behaviours: kinetic type reveals, scroll-scrubbed sections, magnetic buttons, a custom cursor, and a particle flow-field hero.

ease-outDefault for reveals, entrances, hoverscubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1)
ease-inoutLoops, ticker, ambient sweepscubic-bezier(.65,0,.35,1)
springButtons, chips, magnetic pull, popcubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1)
fast 140msTaps, toggles, micro-feedback--dur-fast
base 240msMost transitions--dur-base
slow 420–680msReveals, section transitions, curtains--dur-slow / --dur-slower
Rule
  • Every animation must have a reduced-motion fallback that shows the end-state instantly.
  • Nothing loops purely for decoration where content lives — ambient loops belong to background only.
  • Scroll-driven motion is smoothed through a single shared frame loop, never per-element scroll listeners.

09 — Voice & tone

How Laive speaks

Confident, concrete, and outcome-first. We speak to operators and executives — never to hobbyists. We quantify. We remove hype words and replace them with results.

We sound like

  • Decisive — "Move first." "Outcome as a Service."
  • Quantified — "50–60% ROI", "24/7", "zero new hires".
  • Plain-spoken about value: money, time, errors.
  • Governed & credible — we name real frameworks.

We avoid

  • Hype without proof — "revolutionary", "magical".
  • Jargon for its own sake or feature dumps.
  • Emoji, exclamation stacks, and filler adjectives.
  • Tool-talk before outcome-talk.

11 — In practice

Do & Don't

Do
  • Spend gold on the single most important action per view.
  • Let ink and negative space carry the composition.
  • Use glass + depth to establish hierarchy, not decoration.
  • Quantify claims; lead with outcomes.
  • Ship reduced-motion and keyboard-accessible states.
Don't
  • Fill the screen with gold — it stops being premium.
  • Stack gradients or add glow to everything.
  • Use emoji, or fonts outside the three families.
  • Animate for its own sake or loop over content.
  • Introduce new colors instead of the ink/gold/copper system.
